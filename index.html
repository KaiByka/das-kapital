Der Autor

The Author

A reader in the British Museum, an exile in London, a man who spent decades in the factory reports and blue-books of the age.

1818 · Trier
Born in the Moselle wine country

Third of nine children in a Jewish family converted to the Prussian state church; grows up under the restrictions of Restoration Germany.

1843–1849 · Paris, Brussels, London
Exile and revolution

Expelled from city after city, finally settled in London. With Engels he writes the Communist Manifesto on the eve of the European revolutions of 1848.

1849–1859 · Dean Street, Soho
Poverty and the Grundrisse

Years of hardship: children lost, debts, journalism for the New-York Daily Tribune. At night, in the British Museum's reading room, the manuscripts that will become Capital.

1867 · Hamburg
Volume I published

The first edition of 1,000 copies is printed by Otto Meissner. Marx writes to Engels on the last night of proofing: "So this volume is finished. It was thanks to you alone that this became possible."

1883 · Highgate
"The greatest living thinker has ceased to think"

Eleven mourners at Highgate Cemetery. Engels delivers the eulogy: "Just as Darwin discovered the law of development of organic nature, so Marx discovered the law of development of human history."

Grundbegriffe

The Architecture of the Argument

Each concept in Capital is built dialectically: one leads to the next by an inner necessity, from the simple commodity to the movement of the whole social capital.

W

Ware — Commodity

The cell-form of bourgeois society: at once a use-value and a value, a thing and a social relation in disguise.

G

Gold — Money

The universal equivalent: value's first adequate form of appearance, and the gateway through which value becomes capital.

M

Mehrwert — Surplus-Value

Where does profit come from? From labour-power's peculiar character: it creates more value than it costs to reproduce itself.

K

Kapital — Capital

Not a thing but a process: value in motion, valorising itself — "dead labour that, vampire-like, lives by sucking living labour."

A

Akkumulation — Accumulation

Surplus-value becomes more capital, and more capital demands more labour-power: the system expands its own foundation.

G′

Gesetz — The Law

The general law of capitalist accumulation concentrates and centralises capital — while preparing its own historical limits.
